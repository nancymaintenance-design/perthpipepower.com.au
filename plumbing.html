Practical plumbing information

Start with the symptom, not a guess.

Plumbing problems can appear as a visible drip, a fixture that will not stop running, slow drainage, low pressure, no hot water or moisture where it should not be. A clear description helps place the enquiry with the right information from the outset.

For an active leak, protect people and property only where it is safe to do so. Keep clear of wet electrical equipment, do not remove panels or fittings, and call 000 if there is immediate danger.

Details worth including

Location

What changed

Safe context

Mention any water near electrical equipment, visible damage or spreading water. Do not investigate a hazard simply to provide more detail.

Choose a plumbing service by the symptom

For a slow outlet or wastewater returning through fixtures, start with drainage. Water appearing outside a pipe or fitting belongs with leak assessment; a known damaged pipe belongs with pipe repair. A shower leak may involve plumbing, seals or waterproofing, so the source needs assessment before any repair is agreed. Low pressure at one tap is different from a whole-property supply change. Tell us which outlets are affected rather than assuming that every problem needs pipe replacement.

What does a plumbing quote include?

The written quote identifies the agreed investigation or repair, included materials and relevant access work. The published AUD ranges are guides for standard-hours, standard-scope work, not a fixed quote for an unseen property. Ask which additional work would require further approval.

Can you assess a shower leak or low water pressure?

Describe whether the leak appears during use and which outlets have changed. The assessment separates a plumbing fault from other causes, such as waterproofing or a supply issue. Specialist or additional work is confirmed separately, not assumed from a symptom.
