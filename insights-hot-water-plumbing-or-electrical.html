Is a hot-water problem plumbing or electrical?

By Ellis Services Group · Updated 10 October 2026

At a glance

Record whether one outlet or the whole property has lost hot water, and what has changed in temperature or flow. Water connections and electrical heating equipment can require different work. The assessment determines the relevant trade and repair options; compare repair and replacement quotes against the same included scope.

“No hot water” is a symptom, not a complete diagnosis. The safest starting point is to record what the property is experiencing without opening the unit or touching wiring.

What to observe from a safe position

  • Whether there is no hot water, reduced temperature or fluctuating temperature
  • Whether all outlets are affected or only one
  • System location and any visible water near the unit
  • Whether cold water is normal and other electrical circuits are affected
  • Model information visible on the outside of the unit

Do not remove panels, adjust internal controls or touch a unit that is wet, damaged, smoking or sparking. If there is immediate danger, move away and contact emergency services.

One outlet or the whole property?

A shower that changes temperature while the other hot taps work normally is a different report from a home with no hot water anywhere. A local fixture, water connection and the equipment supplying the property are different parts of the installation. Describe which outlets are affected so the visit starts with the correct scope; a symptom alone does not establish the failed part.

If every hot outlet is affected, tell us whether the water stays cold, becomes warm only briefly or has reduced flow. Cold-water behaviour and the system location are useful context. There is no need to open a unit or investigate wiring to make this distinction.

When plumbing and electrical work overlap

The plumbing side can involve water connections, leaking fittings, supply and outlet behaviour. Electrically heated equipment can also require electrical assessment. Our Perth team coordinates the relevant licensed trades within the agreed scope instead of expecting you to identify the trade from the initial symptom.

Water beside a unit does not prove the storage tank has failed: accessible connections and surrounding pipework also need assessment. Likewise, loss of heating does not automatically mean the entire system needs replacement. Our Perth hot-water repair service explains the assessment and repair options. For moisture whose source is unclear, see water leak detection.

Compare repair and replacement on the same scope

A repair quote should identify the fault-related work and proposed parts or connections. A replacement quote should identify the proposed equipment and the work needed to connect it to the property. Extra pipework, cabling, access and agreed disposal or restoration tasks should not be silently assumed to be included.

Compare written scopes rather than a repair-only price against a complete replacement installation. Ask which items are included, what work is outside the quote and how additional findings will be approved. Published price ranges are planning guides; the final written quote confirms your property-specific work.

Prepare a description, not a repair decision

Say whether hot water is missing at one outlet or throughout the property, whether flow has changed and whether water is visible near the unit. Safely readable product details can help identify the system. Keep gas, refrigeration, electrical and plumbing scope distinct; a general hot-water request does not establish who is authorised for every component.

  • Hot-water repair and replacement assessment
  • Problems isolated to one tap or mixer
Who should I call if I do not know which trade is needed?

Describe the system, affected outlets and current symptoms to the team. The appropriate trade and assessment scope need to be confirmed; you do not need to remove covers or test equipment to choose a category.

Does hot water at one tap rule out a plumbing problem elsewhere?

No. A difference between outlets is useful context but does not establish the faulty component. Report which outlets are affected so the investigation can be scoped.
