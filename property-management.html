Plumbing and Electrical Maintenance for Perth Property Managers

The most useful work order explains the symptom without assuming a diagnosis. “Water appears below the kitchen sink when the tap runs” is more actionable than “replace the pipe”.

What to include

  1. Agency, owner or management contact
  2. Work-order or property reference
  3. Full property address and occupant contact
  4. Symptom, affected area and safe photos
  5. Access, pets, parking or building-entry information
  6. Approval contact and relevant strata details

Rental properties

Ask the occupant to describe what they observed, when it began and whether it remains active. Confirm access and notice requirements.

Strata and shared buildings

Identify whether the issue is inside a lot, in a common area or connected to shared infrastructure.

Commercial premises

Include operating constraints, induction requirements, parking information and the nominated site contact.

Plumbing and Electrical Work Orders: Property, Fault and Approval

Property

Address, unit number, affected area and any strata or common-property context that could shape access.

Issue

The occupant's observable description, when it began, current condition and any safely taken photos.

Access & approval

On-site contact, availability, parking or entry notes, work-order reference and the approval contact.

Tenant Repair Reports and Property-Manager Approval

For rental properties, strata and agency-managed premises, keep the original observation separate from the work instruction. Identify who may approve investigation, who may approve repairs, and whether a spending limit applies. Include the occupant contact and access arrangements without publishing keys, alarm codes or other private entry information.

A hot-water leak and failed bathroom lights can belong in one work order, but they are not one diagnosis. We confirm the relevant plumbing and electrical tasks and quote the agreed scope. For common-property concerns, identify the managing contact rather than assuming that the occupant can authorise shared-system work.

If the condition changes before the visit, update the request. Where you need completion notes, an itemised scope or a particular agency reference, specify that requirement when arranging the work; the required documentation should be agreed, not assumed.

Can an occupant approve extra repairs during the visit?

Access permission and authority to approve cost are different. Give the team the nominated decision-maker, agreed limit and escalation contact. If additional work is needed, its approval follows that instruction rather than assuming the person on site can approve it.

Can one request cover a plumbing and an electrical problem?

Yes. Describe each symptom and location separately and include the same property reference. The team confirms which trades and tasks are required. Coordination does not guarantee one diagnosis, one contractor approval or completion in a single visit.

What information should a strata work order include?

State the affected lot or common area, the managing contact, access arrangements and who can approve the proposed scope. Do not decide responsibility from the location alone; shared infrastructure and approval requirements need to be clarified for that property.

Can I request completion notes or an agency invoice reference?

Include the required records and agency reference when arranging the visit. Agree what documentation will be provided for the defined work. A reference or maintenance receipt does not by itself certify parts of the property outside that scope.

Who should a tenant contact first about plumbing or electrical repairs?

For routine repairs, report to the agent or landlord. For immediate danger, use the appropriate emergency channel first. Read the explanation.

A clear plumbing or electrical work order

Use the same property reference for the occupant report, requested assessment and approval. The example below is an illustrative format, not a completed customer job or a client endorsement. Adapt it to the actual property and confirm the accepted scope with the team.

  • Property: address and unit; identify the affected room or common area.
  • Observation: for example, “the kitchen tap continues to drip when closed”; record what the occupant reported, not an unverified diagnosis.
  • Instruction: assess the stated issue and quote the proposed work; name who may approve investigation and additional repairs.
  • Access: nominated contact and agreed entry arrangements; share keys or private codes through an agreed private channel.
  • Records: state any agency reference, completion notes, photographs or invoice breakdown required, and confirm what will be provided.
