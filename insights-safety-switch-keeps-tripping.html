Electrical guide

What to do when a safety switch keeps tripping

Do not use this checklist for an electrical emergency. For shock or tingling, keep clear and call Western Power 13 13 51; call 000 for a life-threatening emergency or fire. Official reporting guidance.

By Ellis Services Group · Updated 10 October 2026

At a glance

A recurring trip describes a symptom; it does not establish that the safety switch needs replacement. Record the affected areas and what was happening when the trip occurred. The electrician establishes the cause, then explains the investigation, repair and any additional installation work in the quote. Keep clear of wet or damaged electrical equipment.

A safety switch or circuit breaker operates to interrupt power under certain fault or overload conditions. A device that will not remain on, or trips again after being reset, needs attention.

Do not repeatedly reset it

Instead, note which parts of the property lost power and what appliances or equipment were being used before the trip. If something appears damaged, wet, unusually hot or burnt, do not touch or use it.

What to record

  • Label beside the affected switch, if readable without removing a cover
  • Rooms, lights or outlets without power
  • Whether rain, cleaning or water ingress preceded the trip
  • Equipment operating at the time
  • Any sound, smell, heat, smoke or visible damage

Never open a switchboard or work on fixed wiring. If there is smoke, fire, arcing, water near electrical equipment or immediate risk, move clear and call 000.

Contact Ellis Services Group to arrange an on-site assessment. Our qualified electrical service checks the reported trips, identifies the fault and confirms the repair plan and written quote.

A safety switch and a circuit breaker are not the same device

A safety switch or RCD responds to leakage-current imbalance. A circuit breaker provides overcurrent protection. A combined device can perform both functions. See the WA Government RCD safety-switch guidance. The visible label can help describe the equipment, but it does not identify the cause of a trip. Do not remove covers to identify a device or inspect its connections.

Repeated trips can relate to connected equipment or the fixed installation. Weather, water exposure or a particular appliance being in use can be useful context, but a coincidence is not a diagnosis. Report what happened and let the attending electrician establish the fault.

What an electrical assessment needs to establish

The electrician considers the reported trip, affected areas and relevant installation before proposing repairs. Investigating a fault is different from replacing a protective device, repairing fixed wiring or addressing equipment outside the original scope. Our Perth safety switch and RCD repairs page sets out that service pathway.

If the issue is broader loss of power rather than a clearly reported trip, our electrical fault finding and power repairs service is the appropriate related starting point. A whole-property outage and a fault affecting one circuit should be described separately. Do not force the switch on or use equipment that is wet, damaged or smells burnt.

Agree the investigation and repair scope

Ask the written quote to distinguish assessment, the proposed repair, replacement components and additional wiring or installation work. The investigation can identify a different cause from the one suspected at booking. Additional work is explained before it is approved rather than being assumed within an unspecified repair package.

For rental or strata premises, provide the on-site contact, access arrangements and person authorised to approve work. Note whether the affected equipment is private or shared, without entering restricted electrical areas to confirm it. Report a symptom without trying to reproduce it

Useful notes include the affected rooms and whether the trip happened during rain, appliance use or another observed change. These details guide the assessment; they do not prove which part is faulty. Do not repeatedly reset the device, bypass protection or open equipment to produce a better report.

Should I keep resetting the switch to identify the faulty appliance?

No. A recurring trip needs an appropriate electrical assessment. Record what you already observed without repeatedly reproducing the fault, opening equipment or bypassing protection.

Is a tripping safety switch always a problem with the switch itself?

No. The affected installation, connected equipment and protective device may need assessment. A trip alone is not evidence that replacing the switch will resolve the cause.
