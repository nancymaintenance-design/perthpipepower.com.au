# Plumbing and Electrical Service-Page Depth Design

## Purpose

Expand the existing Plumbing and Electrical topic pages so Perth homeowners, property managers and real-estate agencies can understand the symptom they are seeing, provide useful enquiry information and navigate to the relevant related service. The pages must demonstrate practical professionalism without asserting unverified licences, insurance limits, site-specific work or diagnostic outcomes.

## Scope

The existing topic pages are in scope:

- Plumbing: water leak detection, burst pipes, blocked drains, hot-water problems, taps and mixers, toilet repairs, fixtures and appliances.
- Electrical: power faults, safety switches, lighting and power points, smoke-alarm maintenance, renewables and smart home.

The Plumbing and Electrical mega menus will link only to these corresponding topic pages and their service hubs. The contact page and service-area directory remain the conversion and geographic destinations.

## Page structure

Each topic page will use the same reader-first structure, with topic-specific wording:

1. **Problem framing** — recognisable symptoms and the property context.
2. **Safety and boundaries** — immediate hazards, actions not to take and an explicit direction to call 000 in an emergency.
3. **What to include in an enquiry** — address/suburb, affected area, observations, timing, access and approval information.
4. **Professional assessment path** — an accurate high-level description of how information is considered, without guaranteeing a diagnosis or outcome.
5. **Relevant standards and responsible work** — for regulated work, explain that the applicable responsible contractor and current documentation are confirmed before work is arranged. No licence numbers, insurer names, policy limits or certification claims are published unless supplied and verified later.
6. **Related services** — two to four contextually relevant internal links plus service-area and enquiry links.
7. **Topic FAQ** — concise questions that clarify customer actions and scope, without offering unsafe DIY directions.

## EEAT and compliance rules

- Use plain, specific language describing customer-observable symptoms and the information a trade professional needs.
- State that regulated electrical and plumbing work is handled through the appropriate responsible, WA-qualified contracting arrangements; do not imply a particular entity or person holds an unprovided licence.
- Link to the official Western Australian licence-search information where it helps customers verify credentials.
- Do not manufacture testimonials, years of topic-specific experience, prices, response times, inspection findings, completed project claims, certifications, insurance details or manufacturer affiliations.
- Keep emergency wording consistent: immediate danger requires 000; customers must not put themselves at risk for photos or diagnosis.

## Internal-link model

- Each Plumbing page links to the Plumbing hub, two to four closest Plumbing topics, service areas and contact.
- Each Electrical page links to the Electrical hub, two to four closest Electrical topics, service areas and contact.
- The menu remains scannable: topic label, short symptom-led description and hub link.
- Related links are selected manually per topic, not generated from search keywords.

## Acceptance criteria

- All twelve listed topic pages have the seven content blocks or equivalent semantic sections.
- Each page contains at least two relevant topic links, the appropriate hub, service-area directory and contact path.
- All pages retain responsive navigation, mobile CTA and enquiry accessibility.
- New links resolve to existing pages.
- A regression test checks coverage, compliant safety wording and the absence of invented licence/insurance identifiers.
