What makes a useful plumbing or electrical work order

By Ellis Services Group · Updated 10 October 2026

At a glance

List each room, fixture and observed problem separately. Identify who may approve the visit, repairs and additional findings. Ask the quote to distinguish investigation, repair and related access work. Close the record with what was actually completed and any follow-up recommendations; an inspection or recommendation is not a completed repair.

In this guide

  • Keep three details together
  • Describe an assessable task, not an assumed repair
  • Make approval boundaries explicit
  • Close the work order with the actual outcome

A useful work order identifies the property and describes the observable symptom. It does not need to prescribe the repair.

Keep three details together

Under “issue”, record the room, fixture or circuit, the start time and current status. Under “access”, record the occupant or site contact and relevant building instructions. Under “approval”, record the work-order reference and nominated decision-maker.

This structure is useful for a leaking fixture, a slow drain, a tripping circuit or failed lighting because it separates facts from assumptions.

Describe an assessable task, not an assumed repair

Write “kitchen sink drains slowly and backs up during use” rather than instructing a pipe replacement without inspection. Write “bedroom outlets lost power” rather than directing a switchboard replacement from an occupant report. The work order should give the service team an investigation goal while preserving the original observations.

For multiple tasks, record each room, fixture and reported behaviour on its own line. Separate plumbing from electrical items and distinguish faults from planned installation. This prevents a repair quote being mistaken for approval to complete every improvement mentioned in an email.

Make approval boundaries explicit

Identify who can approve the visit, the proposed repair and additional findings. If the owner or manager has an internal approval limit, record it with the work order and explain how further approval can be obtained. An internal limit is not itself a quoted service price.

The written quote should describe inspection, repair or installation tasks and what is excluded. For concealed plumbing, access openings and surface restoration need their own scope. For electrical work, investigation and any new wiring or replacement equipment should be distinguishable. See leak detection and repair scope and electrical fault assessment for service examples.

Close the work order with the actual outcome

Keep the approved quote, work performed and separate follow-up items with the property reference. A recommendation is not a completed task; an inspection is not proof that every related issue was repaired. Make those distinctions clear in the completion record.
