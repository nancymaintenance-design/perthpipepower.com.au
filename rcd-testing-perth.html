Common situations and what to report

Preparing a rental for new occupants

An agent requests RCD and smoke alarm checks during the same visit. Provide the property, access contact and requested checks. We coordinate the work and confirm the record required; an RCD check is not a blanket declaration about every electrical item in the property.

A safety switch does not behave as expected

A device that repeatedly trips or will not reset needs fault assessment, not just a routine testing appointment. Report the symptom when calling so investigation and any repair are scoped appropriately. Do not bypass protection to keep equipment running.

These are illustrative situations, not customer case studies or diagnoses. Report what you have noticed; you do not need to identify the cause before contacting us.

What is the difference between an RCD and a circuit breaker?

An RCD detects an imbalance associated with current leaking from the intended path. A circuit breaker protects against overload or short circuit. A combined device can provide both functions; the devices are not interchangeable terms.

Does a tripping safety switch mean the switch itself is faulty?

No. A trip may indicate a fault in a circuit or connected equipment. The device and installation need assessment before deciding whether the switch, wiring or other equipment requires repair.

Can smoke alarms and RCDs be checked during one visit?

Yes, both can be arranged within an agreed visit. State the checks and records required so the scope, access, interruptions and price are confirmed together.

Is an RCD test the same as a whole-property safety certificate?

No. The record relates to the agreed devices and checks. Confirm the required documentation and any broader inspection separately rather than assuming an RCD test certifies the entire property.
