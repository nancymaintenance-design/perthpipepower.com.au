Reporting a plumbing leak: inspection and repair in Perth

Practical property information

To report a plumbing leak in Perth, send Ellis Services Group the location, timing, water spread and safe access details. We use that information to plan the inspection, investigate the likely source and explain the repair scope before you authorise further work.

You do not need to diagnose a hidden pipe fault before calling. A damp ceiling, water under a cabinet or unexplained meter movement gives us a starting point; the inspection determines what needs attention. Our water leak detection service explains the signs we investigate and how detection and repairs can be scoped separately.

Decide what needs attention first

  • A stable drip or stain you can observe safely: record the affected room, when it started and whether it follows use of a fixture. Send an enquiry with photos taken from a safe position.
  • Active flow or spreading water: call 0413 477 667 and describe whether the flow is continuing and which rooms are affected. If you know the water isolation point and it is safe to reach, isolate the supply. Do not delay contact to collect a full set of photos. Our burst pipe repair page covers active pipe leaks and repair arrangements.
  • Water near electrical fittings, appliances or a switchboard: keep people away and do not touch wet equipment, operate switches in the affected area or enter water to reach an isolation point. Tell us about the electrical hazard when you call so the required trades can be considered. For immediate danger to people, contact emergency services.

Move belongings or contain a small visible drip only where it is safe. Do not remove fittings, open walls or climb into a ceiling to find the source. Access work is part of the scope to discuss with the attending tradesperson.

What to record before the inspection

Start with the address and affected room. Describe where you see water, rather than where you think the pipe is broken: below the kitchen sink, beside the hot-water unit, at the toilet base or on the ceiling below a bathroom. Note whether it is dripping, running or pooling, and whether it appears constantly or only when a fixture is used.

Include the time first noticed, any change in the wet area, staining or swelling, and whether the supply has been isolated. A wide photo can show the location; a closer photo can show the visible detail. Take both without disturbing finishes or approaching electrical hazards. Previous repairs, recent plumbing work and an unusual water bill are useful context, but none proves the cause on its own.

Use water meter records as a clue

If the meter is safely accessible and the situation is stable, note the meter type, readings and times. Water Corporation's household leak-check guidance compares ordinary meter readings after actual water use has stopped for 15 minutes. An integrated smart meter uses its own model instructions and usage display; do not apply the ordinary dial-reading method to every meter.

Record any automatic use that could affect the reading, such as irrigation or an appliance. Meter movement can indicate continuing water use. It does not locate a leak, distinguish every possible source or replace an inspection. Do not postpone help for active flooding or an electrical hazard to run a meter check.

How Ellis takes a report through to a repair scope

1. Confirm the inspection goal and access

We review what you have seen and clarify the affected area, active water flow and any hazard. The visit may need to investigate a suspected supply leak, inspect a visible connection or distinguish plumbing moisture from another source. Confirm the on-site contact, entry arrangements and whether the relevant room, meter and fixtures can be accessed safely.

2. Inspect and explain the findings

The attending tradesperson checks the symptoms and available access, then selects an approach suited to the property and suspected fault. Ask what has been established, what remains uncertain and whether further access or another trade is needed. A suspected area is not the same as a confirmed repair location. We select the investigation method for the suspected fault and available access, and explain any opening work before it proceeds.

Water Corporation's guidance on choosing a plumber for a leak explains why hidden leaks need the appropriate skills. Ellis services are provided by appropriately licensed plumbing and electrical tradespeople; licence details are available on request.

3. Confirm the written scope and approval

Inspection findings lead to a proposed repair, further investigation or related work. Confirm what the quotation includes before authorising that next stage. If opening a wall or floor reveals a different condition, the additional work and cost need to be explained and approved before the scope expands. For a managed property, establish who can approve costs and how urgent findings should be reported.

4. Complete the agreed work and record the outcome

The repair and any agreed checks are completed within the authorised scope. Request a record of the identified fault, work completed and follow-up items. Where another trade must restore a surface or investigate a separate moisture source, keep that task visible in the handover rather than assuming it was included in the pipe repair.

Separate pipe repair from access and surface restoration

A useful quotation makes these items clear:

  • Inspection or detection: the area to investigate, access available and any agreed limit to the initial visit.
  • Access or opening: whether cabinetry, a wall, floor or paving needs to be opened, and who is responsible for that work.
  • Pipe or fitting repair: the identified component, proposed repair and checks included afterward.
  • Surface restoration: whether plaster, paint, tiles, cabinetry or paving reinstatement is included, separately priced or left for another trade.
  • Related work: electrical safety, drying or a separate drainage issue where the inspection identifies a need.

Access, concealed pipe routes, the extent of damage and restoration requirements affect the scope. A quote for finding a leak should not be compared directly with a quote that also includes opening, repair and restoring finishes. For tenants and property managers

Include the tenant or on-site contact, entry permission, suitable access times and the person responsible for approving costs. Note the work-order reference, any approval limit and where photos, findings and invoices should be sent. If a room is unavailable or the meter is behind a locked gate, say so before the visit.

After the work, retain the repair record and any outstanding restoration or reinspection tasks. Our property management service covers work orders, access notes and approval paths for managed Perth properties.

Common leak-reporting questions

Do I need a meter reading before I call?

No. Report what you can see safely. A meter record can help with a stable suspected leak, but active water spread or an electrical hazard takes priority over collecting readings.

Can a photo confirm where the hidden pipe is leaking?

A photo shows visible symptoms and access conditions. Water can travel before it becomes visible, so the inspection must establish the source and whether more access is needed.

Does leak detection include repairing and repainting the wall?

Only if those tasks are included in the agreed scope. Ask for inspection, opening, pipe repair and surface restoration to be identified separately.

What should a tenant send to the property manager?

Send the address, affected room, timing, active flow and safe photos, plus your contact and access availability. Follow the property's urgent-maintenance process when water is continuing to spread.
